Biology Lab Background

Enzyme Kinetics, Inhibition & Denaturation

1. Sequential Starch Hydrolysis

Enzymes lower the activation energy (Ea) of biochemical reactions by binding specific substrates at their three-dimensional active site:

  • Step 1 (Amylase): Cleaves 4-unit Starch polysaccharides into two 2-unit Maltose disaccharides.
  • Step 2 (Maltase): Hydrolyzes each Maltose molecule into two individual Glucose monosaccharides.

Competitive Inhibition

A competitive inhibitor mimics the substrate's shape and binds directly inside the enzyme's active site, physically blocking Starch or Maltose. Because binding is reversible, increasing substrate concentration outcompetes the inhibitor and restores Vmax.

Non-Competitive (Allosteric) Inhibition

A non-competitive inhibitor binds to an allosteric site away from the active site, inducing a conformational shift that warps the active site notch. Adding extra substrate cannot overcome non-competitive inhibition.

Temperature & Irreversible Denaturation

Rising temperature increases molecular collision frequency up to ~37°C–40°C. Above 45°C–50°C (or when Pre-Boiled), thermal energy breaks hydrogen and ionic bonds, permanently unraveling the protein into an inactive polypeptide ribbon.

pH Level & Active Site Charge

Amylase functions best at pH 7.0 while Maltase peaks around pH 6.0–6.5. Extreme H+ or OH− concentrations alter R-group ionization in the active site, reducing substrate affinity and denaturing the enzyme.
